## Notes

- **Domain.** Online co-op in the LBP3 native port: each player runs the port on their own PC and the two games talk through the port's peer link. Vocabulary is in [GLOSSARY.md](../../GLOSSARY.md) ("Online co-op"). This map is separate from the finished [LBP3 native port](../lbp3-port/map.md) map, which ruled online play out of its scope.
- **Who owns what.**
  - The **game netcode** (the lockstep simulation, its messages, its kicks) is the eboot's. The port owns only the layer under the game's sockets: the peer link and the signaling answers.
  - The **online server** (`play.lbppc.com`: shadNet with LBPPC's relay patch, plus Refresh) is LBPPC's. Any change or request on their side is the user's call, and so is creating accounts there. Don't load-test it.
- **Constraints.**
  - **Keep our tick.** The loopback path and the precise epoll timeouts stay byte-for-byte as they are with online off. The gate: Introduction steps ≠ 1 under 1% in two runs, and the Thruster recipe 3/3 ([shadnet_design.md §3](../lbp3-port/best/shadnet_design.md)).
  - **Same version on both sides.** No backwards compatibility: the wire format may break freely, and no fallbacks or mixed-version tests are wanted (the user, 2026-11-15).
  - **Measure before designing.** Nothing here presupposes a rewrite of the link or a library swap.

## Decisions so far

<!-- one line per closed ticket: [title](issues/NN-slug.md): gist -->
- [What did the 15 November session with the friend measure?](issues/01-session-measurements.md): Six connections, all on the relay path at a median RTT of 455 ms, which is the sum of the two players' legs to the server (180 and 279 ms). All three involuntary endings were the game's 10 s kick, and one delay of 9.4 s with no loss came within a second of it. A freeze costs 30 datagrams per frozen second, on either PC. Stalls, the spawn, the wait behind a gap and the longest time the game got nothing aren't logged.
- [Why did no connection with the friend go direct?](issues/02-why-no-direct-path.md): Ranked, not proven. No direct packet arrived on either side, though both had the right addresses and the link probed them once a second throughout. Most likely the friend's NAT gives the link a different mapping toward the host than toward the server, and the host's side only lets in the exact port it sent to. The host's ports suggest its provider shares the public address. The link's rules didn't hold the path on the relay; it lacks a port mapping request, a second outside party to ask, and send logging.
- [What does the game netcode tolerate, and which of its limits can be reached?](issues/03-game-netcode-limits.md): The game netcode has its own reliability layer (acknowledged, ordered streams; a resend after twice its own RTT) and accepts datagrams out of order, so DATA3 hides loss from a layer that already repairs it. The lost datagram that ends a session wasn't found. The kick is a keepalive test: none delivered for 10 s. Guests step on the host's per-frame message. (Its inference that the host doesn't wait for guests was refuted by [What does the host do with a guest's late input?](issues/17-late-guest-input.md).) The port can reach the settings file's prediction, catch-up, loading and timeout keys, the kick limit and resend factor in memory, and the bandwidth it reports; the RTT it reports is never read.
- [Which ways to a direct path work with an online server we can't change?](issues/04-direct-path-options.md): The server offers discovery and a relay, nothing for timing, and the link already punches holes, so timing changes rescue no new pairs. New pairs come from an inbound Windows Firewall rule (this PC has none), a mapped port (UPnP, or by hand) or IPv6. A NAT that maps per destination against one that filters per port stays on the relay path unless both players have IPv6.
- [Is the host's PC behind an address its provider shares, and does it have IPv6?](issues/11-host-address-sharing.md): Yes to both. The home router's outside address is a private one, so the provider's NAT sits behind it and no mapped port on the host's side can be reached. UPnP answers on the router; NAT-PMP and PCP don't. The PC has a global IPv6 address and a working route; the online server has IPv4 only.
- [What does the host do with a guest's late input?](issues/17-late-guest-input.md): The host does wait: it makes no per-frame message while a player has no input queued, for about a second, and nobody steps meanwhile. Gaps in a guest's input set that off (a freeze, a resend, a held burst), not the RTT. Late input is never dropped. A guest's own delay is hidden up to a round trip of 667 ms. The death countdown is most likely the game's rule for a Sackboy left off its own screen, not a network timer.
- [Do the game's own packet delay and drop settings work, and what do the other unread settings do?](issues/16-unread-settings.md): No. The game's packet delay and drop simulator is compiled out of this build, so the test rig's delay, jitter and hold go into the peer link. `EnableKeepAlive` is an HTTP flag and `EnablePrediction` has no reader. The kick limit's float also sets a 9 s threshold for accepting session messages. The link level only feeds a quality figure for display.
- [What makes the game netcode cut its send budget, and does the peer link trigger it?](issues/15-send-budget-cuts.md): A delay that climbs for a second or two cuts it; a steady delay and jitter don't, and the link's bursts seldom do. The game also cuts its own budget when its timed-out resends keep rising. The budget starts at 16,384 bytes a second and doesn't recover in steady play. It limits resends and resource transfer, not first sends: at the floor, 1 MB of resources takes 163 s. Whether a cut dragged the 15 November loads wasn't found.
- [Does a guest that is still loading make the host wait on every frame?](issues/21-loading-guest-wait.md): No. A joining guest enters the host's reckoning only once it is ready, and doesn't leave it at a level change. Every join does begin with one block of 0.8 to 2.4 s in which the host doesn't step (explained by [What stops the host's simulation when a guest starts to join?](issues/23-join-start-stall.md)). The catch-up loading screen can come up after every join and leave.
- [What stops the host's simulation when a guest starts to join?](issues/23-join-start-stall.md): The game's own exchange of the game state: every PC in the session stops until all, the joining guest included, have loaded it, with no time limit. Its length is about 0.8 s plus 4.6 round trips to the joining guest, so about 3 s on the relay path of 15 November and under a second direct. The port can shorten it only through the path.
- [Has the joining-and-leaving lane landed?](issues/25-joining-lane-landed.md): No, and the user said to build on its head as it is: `fix/coop-online` at `54c538f`, each ticket on its own branch from there.
- [How long can a joining guest that hangs keep everyone from stepping?](issues/27-hung-join-blocks-all.md): Up to 10 s when its datagrams stop or its game freezes, ended by the game's own kick; without end if its game runs but never reports the game state loaded, which no timeout covers. Nobody can use a menu meanwhile; closing the window works. The port can end it by saying DEAD on the host's side. The rejoin without a spawn is a different stop.
- [Does a game that thaws after more than 10 s remove every player before it reads what arrived?](issues/29-thaw-removes-everyone.md): No, it reads first and passes its own test. The session is lost all the same, because the PCs that didn't freeze kicked it 10 s in and said so. The port could hold their kick back for a player whose link answers while their game is silent; that costs nothing against our tick.
- [Can a game take part in a join and never report the game state loaded?](issues/28-never-reports-loaded.md): Yes, but only when a resource of the game state goes quiet while the connection stays up; then every PC waits 600 s or more. A fetch that fails ends cleanly within seconds. No cause was shown to happen. An honest join has no fixed longest length (the state's size over the send allowance), so a watchdog would have to go by progress.
- [Can the host's snapshot go away while a guest is still fetching it?](issues/30-snapshot-replaced-mid-join.md): No, by any road found. The snapshot sits in the game's cache file under the hash of its bytes, the host holds a reader on it while it sends, and a second request waits until the first exchange is over. A join that hangs without end has no cause found in honest play.
- [Can the three test accounts sign in and find each other?](issues/24-test-accounts-sign-in.md): Yes. Each instance under `D:\Recomps\lbp3-wt\coop` has its sign-in file, copied from the per-account files in the runtime's `user\config`, and Kistopawa, Kistopawa2 and Kistopawa3 are friends of each other.
- [What does a third player do to a session?](issues/18-third-player.md): The mesh is confirmed and three players hold: ten minutes in the Pod and a level start with no stall and no drift. Simulation data goes through the host; guests only keep each other alive. A guest pair that can't connect keeps the third player out for good, silently: the online server gives addresses to friends only, so a third friend gets in only if they are a friend of every guest already there. A join's block also stops the players already in.
- [Put a set delay and jitter on the peer link between two local instances](issues/05-delay-and-jitter-settings.md): `LBP3_P2P_TEST_DELAY` and `LBP3_P2P_TEST_HOLD`, on the runtime branch `netcode/test-delay`. Two instances at 225 ms each way read an RTT of 450 ms. Steady play at that RTT had no stall at all; the join's block was about 5 s. The tick gate wasn't run and goes with the branch's landing.
- [Which lost datagram ends a session?](issues/13-fatal-datagram.md): None in particular. On a path faster than a frame the port's frame clock can show the game an acknowledgement 100 ns before its message was sent; the game reads that as billions of seconds of RTT for 5 s and resends nothing, so any lost datagram then brings the 10 s kick (3 runs of 3). With negative samples counted as 0, 50 drops in six minutes passed, at the price of an RTT that reads too low and a send budget cut to the floor; at a 60 ms RTT, 33 passed with no fix. It explains the fatal losses of the two-instance runs, not the 15 November kicks. In steady play the game repairs loss itself, so the loss DATA3 was built around isn't the game's; joins, loads and a cut budget are untested.
- [Which log lines does a session need to measure itself?](issues/19-session-log-lines.md): The game's half is built on the runtime branch `netcode/session-log-game`: `[ses]` lines on a change (player slots, the join block, the game state exchange, resources, the catch-up loading screen, a connection's end) and `[ses-win]` and `[ses-conn]` lines every 5 s (steps, the host's waits, each PC's INPUTS, the send budget, the game's RTT beside the link's). Three two-instance runs and one with the probes off wrote them all, and the tick gate holds with online off (0.26% and 0.46%, Thruster 3 of 3). Never seen above zero: a drop at the socket queue, and resource bytes inside a join block. The second outside party went to the NAT ticket and the launcher's report to a ticket of its own.
- [What are the entries the host thread keeps awaiting after a join?](issues/34-awaited-entries-after-join.md): Data labels entries, not game state ones: the host thread makes a pair for the labels message that follows every exchange and every level load with guests, and nobody reports them loaded when the message carries no labels. Nothing waits on them and they cost 80 bytes each. The session log's exchange line names them wrongly, and a watchdog must skip them.
- [How does today's online co-op hold up at set delays and loss?](issues/06-coop-under-set-conditions.md): Steady delay up to a 1000 ms RTT and single losses up to 5% don't break it, with gap-free delivery on or off: they cost waiting (the join's block is 4 s at 450 ms, a level start stalls everyone a second). What breaks it first is loss in bursts on a slow path: at 450 ms, three datagrams lost in a row out of every 101 leave the guest without a step on a third of its frames, four on 40%. Gap-free delivery covers a burst of two with its copies (without it two in a row already break play) and nothing longer. A guest's freeze stalls everyone for up to a second, a third of a second with the catch-up window at 10. A 12 s freeze survives with the kick limit at 30 s.
- [What is the netcode bar?](issues/07-netcode-bar.md): 18 targets, each a keep (today's build does it on the rig; don't make it worse) or a gain (what 15 November asks for), with its cell and a pass rule of two ten-minute runs. Playable means no stall of half a second, under 10% of flips with no step and no catch-up loading screen over play, read from step counts and still to be confirmed by feel. The gains: direct where a rung exists and ESTABLISHED in 2 s, no kick from loss on a fast path, another player's freeze costing 0.4 s, 12 s of silence survived, four lost in a row playable at 450 ms. A relay path at 450 ms passes when no rung serves the pair. One session with the friend checks it on a real path. The keeps rest on single short runs until [Does today's build pass the bar's keep targets?](issues/37-bar-baseline.md) is run.
- [How does the port keep the game's RTT right on a path faster than a frame?](issues/32-rtt-on-fast-paths.md): Both, as two build steps. First an RTT sample under one frame counts as one frame: run once at no added delay with gap-free delivery off, 567 drops in six minutes, no kick and no budget cut. The true round trip is the wrong value there, since one acknowledgement in seven takes a frame longer and the game resends after twice its RTT. Then one clock for the game's connection code, picked out by the caller's address, because its resend timer and other timers misread a negative difference the same way; that step carries the tick gate and wasn't tried.

## Not yet specified

- **A nearer relay, or our own.** The relayed RTT is the sum of the two players' legs to LBPPC's server (180 and 279 ms on 15 November), so a relay nearer to both would cut it. The host sits behind its provider's NAT ([Is the host's PC behind an address its provider shares, and does it have IPv6?](issues/11-host-address-sharing.md)), so for this pair only a punched hole or IPv6 can avoid the relay. Whether any pair needs a nearer one hangs on [Does an inbound Windows Firewall rule on both PCs open a direct path to the friend?](issues/20-firewall-rule-test.md), [Which NAT gives the link a mapping per destination: the friend's, the host's or both?](issues/12-which-nat.md) and [How do two players get a direct path, and what carries them when they can't?](issues/08-path-ladder.md). The bar counts a relay path at 450 ms as passing when no rung can serve a pair ([What is the netcode bar?](issues/07-netcode-bar.md)), so it doesn't call for a nearer relay by itself; the user may see that differently. Hosting costs money: this is where a question for the user is likely.
- **Loads.** Joins and level changes send up to three times the usual traffic while the guest goes quiet. A loading guest isn't waited for as such ([Does a guest that is still loading make the host wait on every frame?](issues/21-loading-guest-wait.md)); its game does stop sending input for 1.2 to 3 s at a level start, and everyone stands still for about a second of it ([How does today's online co-op hold up at set delays and loss?](issues/06-coop-under-set-conditions.md)). The block with no step at the start of a join is the game's own exchange of the game state ([What stops the host's simulation when a guest starts to join?](issues/23-join-start-stall.md)); in the Introduction the state is 310 KB and the block is no longer than in the Pod; a big community level and a cut send budget weren't tried, nor what the 0.8 s on a direct path is made of, and both want the log lines first. A hung joining guest holds everyone for up to 10 s, or without end ([How long can a joining guest that hangs keep everyone from stepping?](issues/27-hung-join-blocks-all.md)); whether the port should cut such a wait short is now part of [Which of the game netcode's own values does the port change?](issues/14-game-netcode-values.md); the likeliest way into such a wait was ruled out ([Can the host's snapshot go away while a guest is still fetching it?](issues/30-snapshot-replaced-mid-join.md)). A cut send budget slows resource transfer to about 6 KB a second ([What makes the game netcode cut its send budget, and does the peer link trigger it?](issues/15-send-budget-cuts.md)); whether that happened in a real load can now be read from the `[ses-conn]` lines of a session ([Which log lines does a session need to measure itself?](issues/19-session-log-lines.md)). Whether the link should pace or favour anything during loads waits on both. In a join into the Introduction the state was seen in the guest's download place inside the block.
- **What helps with loss in bursts.** Gap-free delivery as built covers two in a row and no more: past that it holds a round trip, pushes the game's RTT up and sends each missing datagram again 13 to 14 times ([How does today's online co-op hold up at set delays and loss?](issues/06-coop-under-set-conditions.md)). Sending each datagram twice, spreading copies over more datagrams, or leaving bursts to the game are candidates nobody has tried. It belongs to [How should the peer link deliver game datagrams?](issues/09-datagram-delivery.md), which may want a run before it decides.
- **What the joining-and-leaving lane leaves open.** Reconnecting after the online server's connection drops, anything its rejoin work doesn't settle, and why the host's game ended two connections within 3 to 5 s of their coming up on 15 November (connections 4 and 5, on call paths other than the kick's).
- **Checking the bar on a real internet path.** A session with the friend on their PC. [What is the netcode bar?](issues/07-netcode-bar.md) says what it checks (30 minutes, two joins, two level starts, scored from both session logs, and the user's word on feel). It can't be planned until there is a build.
- **Resources from other players aren't filtered.** What a game fetches from another player bypasses the port's download filter (`CheckDownload`), which applies to HTTP only ([Can a game take part in a join and never report the game state loaded?](issues/28-never-reports-loaded.md)). Whether that belongs to this map, to the joining-and-leaving lane or to its own effort is the user's call.
- **Is the rejoin without a spawn a transfer that is ignored?** In two rejoin runs (rt2, rt4) the guest's record in the game's other transfer manager never drains, and the host ignores a request that matches a transfer it has under way ([Can the host's snapshot go away while a guest is still fetching it?](issues/30-snapshot-replaced-mid-join.md)). That bug is the joining-and-leaving lane's; this is a lead for it.

## Out of scope

- **Rewriting the simulation's netcode** (rollback, prediction, a different tick). It is the eboot's; the port runs the game's own code.
- **Changing LBPPC's online server** (its relay, signaling or matchmaking), and **Dive In with strangers**, which that server answers empty.
- **Playing with LBPPC's own client.** The two ports' peer links don't share a wire format; making them meet would be its own effort.
- **Mixed versions.** Both players run the same release.
- **Voice chat.**
- **Local co-op on one PC.** It isn't netcode; its loopback path is a constraint here, not a subject.
- **Building the rework.** It starts from the spec.
